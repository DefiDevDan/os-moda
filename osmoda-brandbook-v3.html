01 Brand at a glance Positioning · purpose · principles
What we do

osModa is an agentic operations platform.

We deploy and manage a workforce of specialized AI agents — eight purpose-built workers that handle inbox, books, intelligence, archive, background jobs, coordination, key management, and policy enforcement.

Our customers run osModa as a self-hosted server or in our managed cloud. They connect their tools, configure policy, and authorize the gateway token. From that point on, the agents work continuously: they take requests, draft outputs, escalate risky actions to a human, and hand work off to one another via a structured task ledger.

Every output is auditable. Every action is governed by policy. Every key stays inside KEYD, our air-gapped key-management module — covered in detail in chapter 09.

Specialized, not general

Eight agents, one role each.

We don't ship a single general-purpose model. Each agent has a fixed role, a tool surface, and a hand-off contract. Specialization makes them faster, cheaper, and easier to audit.

Auditable by default

Every action leaves a paper trail.

Tasks, hand-offs, escalations, and stamps are written to an immutable ledger. Compliance teams can reconstruct any decision. SOC 2 Type II in audit.

Secure where it matters

KEYD keeps the keys.

Wallet keys, API secrets, and signing material live behind an air-gapped boundary with AES-256-GCM lockboxes, allowlists, and daily limits. Naga refuses what crosses the line.

A note on our internal language

We name our agents after spirits. It's a working metaphor — not the headline.

Inside the company, we talk about Tofu, Frog, Heron, Lantern, Soot, Haku, Naga, and Paper. Each name is a stable codename for a stable role — the way Apple ships under fruit codenames or Anthropic ships Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku. Customers see the codenames in the product UI; they're memorable, friendly, and they reduce ambiguity in operations and incident reports.

In sales and marketing material, lead with the role first ("the inbox agent") and the codename second ("Tofu"). In product UI, the codename can lead. Never use the metaphor to obscure what something does. Specifics over poetry.

02 Logo & mark Wordmark · symbol · clear space
The wordmark

osModa

The wordmark sets os in Inter at weight 600 and pairs it with Moda in Instrument Serif italic. It is the only place in the system where the serif touches the sans this directly — a deliberate signal that we are technical and considered, not one or the other.

Always lowercase. Never break the join. Never substitute the italic — Instrument Serif is non-negotiable here. The accent color is reserved for hero placements; default to single-color black or white.

osModa
The symbol

A pixel bathhouse.

The symbol is a pixel-rendered bathhouse — three tiered roofs, lit windows, a bridge across the bottom. It is the only place where pixel art carries the brand alone. Use it as an app icon, favicon, or product avatar.

Always rendered with crisp pixel edges (shape-rendering: crispEdges). Never anti-aliased. Never re-drawn in vector smooth curves. The pixel grid is the point.

Sizes

Minimum digital: 24px. Minimum print: 8mm. Below this size, use the wordmark instead.

03 Color system 15 tokens · neutral-led · single warm accent

Neutral surfaces. One warm accent. Roles for the rest.

Keep the ratio. Surface-to-ink-to-accent should land near 72:22:6. When in doubt, remove a color.

04 Typography Inter · Instrument Serif · JetBrains Mono · Press Start 2P
Workhorse

Inter.

Inter does the heavy lifting. It is the most-researched UI typeface available open-source, with extensive character coverage, OpenType features for tabular numerals, and hinted screen rendering that holds up at 12px. We use weights 400, 500, 600, 700.

Always tighten tracking on display sizes (-0.025em or tighter). At body sizes, hold tracking at default. Activate cv11, ss01, ss03 for our preferred letterforms.

08 Voice & tone Direct · technical · honest about uncertainty
Principles

Specifics over poetry. Numbers over adjectives.

osModa is a B2B platform sold to operations, finance, and security teams. They are technically literate, time-poor, and skeptical of AI marketing language. We earn their trust by being concrete.

  • 01
    Lead with the role.

    "The inbox agent" before "Tofu". Codenames are internal language — they appear in product, not in pitches.

  • 02
    Show the number.

    "99.95% uptime SLO" beats "highly available". "8.4s average response" beats "fast."

  • 03
    Name the limit.

    Be honest about what the agent doesn't do, what gets escalated, and where the policy boundary sits.

  • 04
    Skip the metaphor in headlines.

    "Bathhouse" stays internal. Customer-facing copy says "platform", "workforce", "deployment".

  • 05
    No exclamation marks.

    If the sentence needs an exclamation mark to land, the sentence isn't ready.

09 KEYD · key management module Security architecture · air-gapped signing
Module

KEYD keeps the keys.

KEYD is the security boundary inside osModa. It generates and stores wallet keys, API secrets, and signing material in air-gapped lockboxes. The signing machine has no public network. Transactions enter as scrolls; signed scrolls leave through couriers. Naga refuses what crosses policy.

KEYD · system illustration · v0.1

Illustration: the KEYD module rendered as a Thai-mountain cave factory. Each labeled chamber maps to a real subsystem — ETH and SOL wallet shrines (key storage), AES-256-GCM lockboxes, the air-gapped signing room (no internet), the policy engine alcoves, allowlist and daily-spend-limit displays, and the courier exit for signed transaction scrolls. The illustration is canonical: feel free to crop, but never alter the labels.

Security architecture

AES-256-GCM lockboxes

Wallet keys live inside hardware-bound lockboxes with authenticated encryption. Decrypt only at the moment of signing. Rotated quarterly; emergency rotate in under 5 minutes.

Sealed

Air-gapped signing

The signing machine has no public network interface. No DNS resolution. No outbound HTTP. Transaction requests enter as serialized payloads through a one-way bridge; signed payloads leave the same way.

No internet

Policy engine

Every transaction is evaluated against the policy ledger before signing. Destination allowlists, daily spend limits, time-of-day windows, and mandatory-stamp thresholds. Refusals are logged and explainable.

Enforced
Threat model — what KEYD defends against
T1 Compromised application server Keys never leave KEYD; ops gateway only carries signed payloads
T2 Network intrusion / lateral movement Air-gapped signer; no inbound or outbound public network on signing host
T3 Insider with stolen credentials Policy engine enforces allowlist + daily limit independently of caller identity
T4 Prompt-injected agent attempts unauthorized transfer Same as T3 — policy enforcement is post-agent, pre-sign
T5 Hardware tamper / physical access HSM-bound keys; tamper-evident enclosures; quarterly rotation

11 Misuse & restrictions Six rules · with examples
Rules

Six things never to do.

The brand has a small number of hard restrictions. Most exist to protect the wordmark, the metaphor boundary, and the security posture of KEYD. When in doubt, ask the brand team.

Never

Don't substitute Instrument Serif

"Moda" must always be set in Instrument Serif italic. Substituting another serif (Times, Georgia, Playfair) breaks the wordmark contract.
Always

Use the licensed wordmark file

Use the SVG provided in /brand/wordmark.svg. Never re-typeset the wordmark from scratch in design tools.
Never

Don't lead with bathhouse metaphor

"Welcome to the bathhouse" headlines on customer-facing pages weaken the platform claim. The metaphor is internal language only.
Always

Lead with role and outcome

"Inbox triage at scale" or "Air-gapped key management" front-loads what the platform does. Codenames belong in product UI and operations.
Never

Don't use gold outside KEYD

Gold (#C8902A / #E4A93A) is reserved for KEYD-related surfaces. Using it on non-vault UI dilutes the security signal.
Always

Use coral for general emphasis

Coral is the workhorse accent. Use it for stamps, denials, attention pulls, mid-sentence emphasis. Sparingly — keep the 4% ratio.
Never

Don't anti-alias pixel art

Pixel illustrations rendered with smooth interpolation lose their identity. Always use shape-rendering: crispEdges or equivalent.
Always

Render at integer scales

Pixel art at 16, 32, 48, 64, 96. Never 18, 26, 50. Off-grid scaling produces blurred sub-pixels.
Never

Don't alter KEYD signage

The labels in the KEYD cave illustration (KEYD, ETH WALLET, SOL WALLET, AES-256-GCM, etc.) are canonical. Don't translate, abbreviate, or restyle them.
Always

Crop, don't redraw

Cropping the KEYD illustration is fine. Redrawing chambers, swapping signs, or recoloring is not. Use the canonical SVG.
Never

Don't claim what we don't ship

No "fully autonomous", no "AGI-grade", no "self-improving". The agents are specialized, governed, and audit-bound. Be specific.
Always

Cite what we measure

SLO, response time, accuracy on internal benchmarks, reconciliation precision. Cite the source. Date-stamp the figure.
12 Colophon Production · maintenance · the metaphor
Production

How this book was made.

Set in Inter (Rasmus Andersson, 2017–) for body and display, Instrument Serif (Rodrigo Fuenzalida) for the wordmark italic, and JetBrains Mono for code and meta. Pixel illustrations rendered in raw SVG with crisp pixel edges; in-scene labels typeset in Press Start 2P (CodeMan38).

Built in a single HTML document. No bundler, no framework. The brand book is a static artifact by design — it should outlive whatever tooling we use to ship it.

Internal language — the bathhouse metaphor

For the record.

Inside the company we draw on a working metaphor borrowed from Hayao Miyazaki's Spirited Away — Yubaba's bathhouse as a workshop where specialized spirits each do one thing well, sign contracts on entry, and walk out with their names intact. The metaphor was useful in early product design and we still use it for naming.

It's not the headline. It's the working language.
